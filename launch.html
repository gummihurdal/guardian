Launch plan, 13 October 2026

From idea to €1,000 a month in 90 days.

Beside a full-time job, with under €5,000 and no more than ten hours a week. Everything below is sequenced so that no money is spent before the gate is cleared, and no building happens that a pilot customer has not asked for.

The gate: employer clearance

One thing comes before everything. Ask HR or compliance this week, in writing, for permission to run a side business: a non-intrusive monitoring and reporting service for small companies, with no overlap with the bank's suppliers or clients, no use of bank systems, time or information, and no security work that could be read as testing or attacking. Until the written answer is in, build only in your own lab and sell nothing.

The pitch in one sentence, for every conversation from here on: "Guardian proves every month that your backups would restore and that nothing of yours is exposed on the internet, in a report your insurer and your auditor accept."

Stack: Python, Postgres, one Hetzner server in Helsinki or Nuremberg, systemd timers, Claude API for report writing, a static status page. No Kubernetes, no SaaS platform, nothing you have not run before. Everything already matches what you operate at work and on your own servers.

What you never build: a customer dashboard before month four, a mobile app, a billing platform, integrations nobody has asked for.

Step 3 — Two pilot customers, weeks 3–8

Pilots are how the report format gets fixed. Two is enough; they must be real companies with real backups, and they should be different from each other: one running Veeam directly, one through an IT provider.

  • Who to ask: former colleagues from Norway, Austria or Switzerland who now run or manage IT at a company with 20 to 200 staff; a friend's company; the IT provider of a business you already know. Ask three, expect two.
  • The deal: free for the first month, then €199 a month or a frank reason why not. Written scope and authorisation signed before the first scan, even for friends.
  • What they get: the first report by hand, walked through on a call, and the question "what would you change?" Their answers become the template.
  • What you get: two named references, two quotes for the site, and the first evidence that the report answers a real insurer or auditor question.

Step 4 — Selling, from week 8

Selling is the key dependency in the score, so it gets a method, not hope. Targets: five paying by day 90, fifteen by day 180, thirty by day 365.

Channels, in order

  1. Your own network, directly. Thirty personal messages to people you know who run or influence IT at small companies. Expect one in six to take a call and one in three calls to convert. That is the first five customers.
  2. IT providers (MSPs). The multiplier: one provider brings ten sites. Find twenty in Zürich, Aargau, Basel and Bern; offer the Provider plan with their name on the reports. Pitch: "your customers' insurers ask for evidence you cannot easily produce; we produce it under your brand."
  3. Insurance brokers. Five brokers who place cyber cover for SMEs. They lose deals when clients cannot answer the questionnaire. Offer the Compliance tier as the thing that gets their client insured.
  4. Content. One plain-language article a month, in German and English, starting with the October 2026 freezer attack: what happened, what it means for a shop or a clinic, five things to check this week. Posted on LinkedIn and the site; no paid ads before month six.

The call, in ten minutes

  1. Ask what their backup system is and when they last restored something on purpose. Most cannot answer the second question.
  2. Ask whether their insurer or a customer has sent a security questionnaire in the last year. Most say yes.
  3. Show one page of a sample report with the two red rows: restore test older than 90 days, one device reachable from the internet.
  4. Offer the Company plan, first month free if they sign the authorisation this week.

Objections and answers

"Is this a security scan? We do not want anything broken.""Non-intrusive only: public data and read-only connections. We never log in, never test, never change anything. It is in the contract."
"€199 is a lot for a report.""A consultant's annual evidence costs €5,000 and is out of date the next day. This is monthly, and it is what your renewal depends on."

Operations — under two hours per customer per month

  • Onboarding, 90 minutes once: signed scope, read-only account created by the customer, device list received, first scan reviewed by hand, welcome call.
  • Monthly, 45 minutes: read the generated report, correct anything Claude got wrong, send it on the first working day of the month.
  • Alerts, as they come: a same-day email; you never fix anything yourself, you tell them what to fix and who should do it.
  • Quarterly, 30 minutes: sign the attestation.
  • Support: email only, answered within one working day, from a dedicated address, never from your work phone.

At thirty customers that is roughly 25 hours a month, inside the ten-hour-a-week limit. Beyond forty, hire a part-time analyst or stop adding Company customers and grow only through Providers.

Kill criteria and risks

Decide in advance what would stop the project, so the decision is not made in a tired moment.

  • Stop if the employer says no in writing: Guardian waits, or is sold to a partner who runs it.
  • Stop if neither pilot converts to paid by 15 December and cannot say what would have changed their mind.
  • Stop if fewer than three customers pay by 31 January 2027 after all thirty messages and ten calls.
  • Stop if monthly delivery takes more than two hours per customer by month four; the model is not working.
